**Read this before relying on it.** Every row names Luna because every variant that passed a
purpose tied on cost (codex reports none), and the rules then take the lower effort and, between
families at the same effort, the variant name. The implement row rests on two tasks and one
repetition, with Luna at medium failing one of the two while Luna at low passed it, which is
the kind of single-run flip repetitions exist to catch. The AdaNext allocation keeps Sol at
medium effort for implementation; a run that names any of backend, model or effort is never
routed, so that allocation stands wherever it is written down. This table is evidence that
Luna passed five of six tasks at low effort and the sixth at medium, not that Luna is as good
as Sol.
